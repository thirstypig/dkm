Services

Two ways to work together

Ongoing talent producing for a series, or focused booking for a single project. Either way, the goal is the same: the right guest, ready to deliver.

01

Talent Producing

Fractional · week over week

The guest side of a show is continuous work — pipeline, outreach, scheduling, prep — and it rarely justifies a full-time hire on a lean production. Donyell runs it for you as a fractional talent producer, credited as Talent Producer.

What you get each week

  • Weekly guest strategy
  • A forward-looking guest pipeline
  • Tailored pitches to representation
  • Scheduling against your production calendar
  • A prep document for every booked guest
  • A shared booking tracker
  • A weekly status call

Best for: podcasts, interview shows, recurring and creator-led series.

02

Talent Booking

Per project

Sourcing, vetting, and booking talent for a defined project, campaign, or event — drawing on direct relationships with talent, managers, and publicists built over a career in network television.

Best for: specials, campaigns, brand activations, festivals, summits, and casting teams that need overflow capacity.

Scope

Talent fees, travel, and production costs are the client’s. Deal negotiation and talent representation are outside the scope of these services.

How she works

Bookings fail
in two ways

She’s hired to prevent both.

01

The audience doesn’t care

The guest looks right on paper, but isn’t the person this audience wants to hear from. Most clients can make a reasonable guess here.

02

The guest doesn’t deliver

Almost no one can judge this before the talent is in the room — the guest who arrives unprepared, freezes when the conversation goes off-script, is difficult with the crew, or talks for thirty minutes without saying anything usable.

The weekly rhythm

What a week looks like

For talent producing engagements, every week runs on the same seven pieces — so you always know who’s coming, when, and that they’re ready.

  1. 01

    Guest strategy

    Who the show needs next, and why.

  2. 02

    Forward pipeline

    A running list of targets, weeks ahead of the calendar.

  3. 03

    Tailored pitches

    Outreach written for each guest’s representation, not a form letter.

  4. 04

    Scheduling

    Bookings locked against your production calendar.

  5. 05

    Guest prep document

    One per booked guest, so hosts and producers walk in ready.

  6. 06

    Shared tracker

    Every pitch, hold, and confirmation in one place you can see.

  7. 07

    Weekly status call

    A standing check-in on what’s booked, pending, and next.

The asset

Her read on who is the best overall fit — for the audience, and for the room.

About

Donyell Kennedy-McCullough

Former SVP, Artist & Talent Relations Strategy and Casting

Donyell Kennedy-McCullough has spent more than fifteen years on the talent side of television. Most recently she was Senior Vice President of Talent Relations and Casting at REVOLT Media & TV, overseeing talent relations and casting for the network’s original programming.

Before REVOLT, she spent seven years as Head of Talent and Casting at TV One, setting talent strategy across the network’s scripted and non-scripted series. Earlier, she booked guests and talent for non-scripted productions at BET and for Telepictures’ Just Keke.

Today she brings that experience — and a network of talent, managers, and publicists who return her calls — to producers, networks, brands, and creators. She is based in Los Angeles. She is also a published author and photographer, and a board member of Black Girls Film Camp.
